# 🐉 From Stone Monkey to Saiyan: Wu Cheng'en, *Journey to the West*, and Akira Toriyama

How a sixteenth-century Chinese novel became the skeleton of the best-selling manga *Dragon Ball*, and what Toriyama kept, cut, and invented along the way.

---

## At a Glance

| | 🏯 Wu Cheng'en (吴承恩) | 🖌️ Akira Toriyama (鳥山明) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lifespan** | c. 1500–c. 1582, Ming Dynasty China | April 5, 1955 – March 1, 2024, Kiyosu, Aichi, Japan |
| **Work** | *Journey to the West* (西遊記), traditionally attributed to him | *Dragon Ball* (ドラゴンボール) |
| **Hero** | Sun Wukong, the Monkey King | Son Goku |
| **Premise** | A pilgrimage to India to fetch Buddhist scriptures, through 81 tribulations | A quest to gather seven wish-granting Dragon Balls, then a series of martial-arts tournaments and world-ending threats |
| **Medium** | Vernacular Chinese novel in 100 chapters | Weekly manga: 519 chapters in 42 volumes (1984–1995), plus anime and games |
| **Tone** | Satire, Buddhist and Taoist allegory, rebellion against bureaucracy | Comedy, martial arts, escalating power |

> **A note on attribution.** *Journey to the West* was published anonymously in the 1590s. Wu Cheng'en is its traditional author, and scholars still debate it. The novel itself grew out of centuries of folk tales about the monk Xuanzang's real seventh-century journey to India.

## Timeline: How Dragon Ball Came to Be

| Date | Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1977–78** | Toriyama, a former graphic designer from Nagoya, sends manga to *Weekly Shōnen Jump*; editor Kazuhiko Torishima rejects roughly 500 pages before his 1978 debut. |
| **Oct 6, 1981** | Toriyama gets engaged to Nachi Mikami (pen name of Yoshimi Katō), a former shōjo manga artist. |
| **Apr 20, 1982** | The Tsun family appears in *Dr. Slump*, his first characters with a Chinese motif. |
| **May 2, 1982** | Toriyama and Mikami marry. |
| **Dec 1982** | He begins to want to draw a kung-fu comic, shaped by his love of Jackie Chan's *Drunken Master* and Bruce Lee's films. |
| **Jun–Oct 1983** | *Dragon Boy* runs in *Fresh Jump* (Part 1 in the August 1983 issue, Part 2 in October), a kung-fu one-shot that serves as a testing ground. |
| **Nov 29, 1983** | *The Adventure of Tongpoo* runs in *Weekly Shōnen Jump* issue 52, an artifact-hunting adventure with capsules, a girl inventor, and a pig. |
| **Spring 1984** | *Dr. Slump* wraps up (issue 39 of 1984). Toriyama travels to China on a research trip. |
| **Nov 20, 1984** | *Dragon Ball* chapter 1, "Bulma and Son Goku," goes on sale in *Weekly Shōnen Jump* issue 51 (cover date December 3, 1984). |
| **1995** | *Dragon Ball* ends; the magazine's circulation peaks at 6.53 million. |

Two details from this timeline shaped the series. His first, near-faithful take on the Journey to the West story reportedly got a lukewarm reaction, including from his wife. And Torishima pushed for a faster, less comic, action-driven manga for *Jump*'s young male readers. Toriyama has said he settled on *Journey to the West* as the foundation himself, while the kung-fu direction came from his editor and from his own film obsession.

## 🔄 The Narrative Lineage

Toriyama did not copy Wu Cheng'en. He took the novel's outline and rebuilt it for modern readers.

### Character equivalencies

| *Journey to the West* | *Dragon Ball* | What changed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sun Wukong**, the stone-born Monkey King with a magic staff (*Ruyi Jingu Bang*) and a cloud that flies (*Jindou Yun*) | **Son Goku**, a tailed boy with the extending Power Pole (*Nyoibo*) and the Flying Nimbus (*Kinto'un*) | Goku is innocent and cheerful rather than a rebel against Heaven. The name "Son Goku" is the Japanese reading of Sun Wukong's name. |
| **Tang Sanzang**, the gentle monk whose journey drives the plot | **Bulma**, a brilliant teenage inventor who sets the quest in motion | The vulnerable leader becomes a sharp, tech-savvy girl with a radar. |
| **Zhu Bajie**, the greedy shapeshifting pig | **Oolong**, a cowardly shapeshifting pig | The shapeshifting stays; the temperament shifts toward comic cowardice. |
| **Sha Wujing**, the sand demon | **Yamcha**, a desert bandit | The desert setting carries over; the menace turns into a rivalry and then friendship. |

Later characters echo the source too: Master Roshi's training and the Kame House, the Ox-King, Goku's fall in strength and rebirth in each arc, and the repeated pattern of enemies who become allies.

### Structural metamorphosis

Wu Cheng'en's pilgrims move from trial to trial until they reach their goal. Early *Dragon Ball* follows the same episodic road, with new companions, a town to save, and a monster to beat. Then Toriyama changes the engine. After the World Martial Arts Tournament proved popular with readers, the series pivots to training, tournaments, and ever-larger fights. The journey becomes a ladder.

## ✍️ Collaboration at Home

Toriyama's wife, Nachi Mikami, shared his interest in Chinese settings, and her influence shows up in small but famous ways. By Toriyama's own account, she suggested the name **Kamehameha** for Goku's signature attack, as a joke. Toriyama kept it.

## ⚔️ Direct Stylistic Comparison

### Creative philosophy and medium

- **Wu Cheng'en** worked in vernacular Chinese prose, mixing poetry with colloquial speech. The novel satirizes the celestial bureaucracy as a mirror of imperial officialdom, and it layers Buddhist, Taoist, and Confucian ideas on top of a comic adventure.
- **Toriyama** worked in sequential art. He stripped away heavy dialogue and philosophy in favor of clear silhouettes, kinetic action, and brisk pacing. He is open about how he kept effort low: invented settings so he would not need reference material, flight to skip travel scenes, and a plain, recognizable design for every major character. He also insisted that fights escalate, because "if you draw it on the same level as the previous fight, it'll seem to the readers that the level has actually gone down."

### Evolution of the hero

- **Sun Wukong** begins at the peak of his power, rebels against Heaven, and is punished. His arc is about **discipline, restraint, and enlightenment** under a master.
- **Son Goku** begins as a country boy with a tail and no ambitions. His arc is about **growth, training, and the pure joy of fighting a stronger opponent**. He never seeks enlightenment. He seeks the next challenge.

### The East Asian cinema bridge

Toriyama did not read the novel and write a manga. Between the book and the page stands a century of screen adaptations, plus Hong Kong martial-arts cinema. *Drunken Master* in particular, which he has said he watched dozens of times, shaped Goku's training scenes and Master Roshi's humor. Toriyama's version of the Monkey King is as much Jackie Chan as Wu Cheng'en.

## 🌍 Legacy

```
[Wu Cheng'en: Journey to the West, 1590s]
        │
        ▼  folk tales, opera, film, and TV adaptations
[Toriyama: Dragon Ball, 1984–1995]
        │
        ▼  the shōnen template: training, tournaments, rivals, power-ups
[One Piece, Naruto, Bleach, My Hero Academia, and global pop culture]
```

*Journey to the West* gave East Asian fantasy its most famous cast and its most durable structure: a band of flawed travelers, a long road, and a trial at every stop. *Dragon Ball* turned that structure into a weekly engine for young readers, and a generation of artists, including Eiichiro Oda and Masashi Kishimoto, have named it as a formative influence. Oda's tribute at Toriyama's death compared him to a big tree.

On March 8, 2024, when his studio announced his death, fans gathered from Buenos Aires to Paris to perform Goku's Spirit Bomb pose together. No one needed it translated. It is still a monkey boy's gesture, four hundred years after the first one.

## Quick Reference

- **Born:** Akira Toriyama, April 5, 1955, Kiyosu, Aichi Prefecture
- **Died:** March 1, 2024, at age 68, of an acute subdural hematoma
- **Series length:** 519 chapters, 42 collected volumes
- **Estimated sales:** about 260 million copies worldwide per his studio's 2024 count, with roughly 159.5 million in Japan
- **Source novel:** *Journey to the West*, 100 chapters, 81 tribulations
